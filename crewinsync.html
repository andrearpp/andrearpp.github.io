CrewInSync — Redesigning a startup product in 50 hours

CrewInSync needed a much stronger product experience, but the design effort had to stay realistic for an early-stage startup. The constraint was part of the problem, not something to ignore.

AI recruitment~50 hoursDesign system
In one sentence

A deliberately constrained engagement focused on key flows, visual direction and a reusable foundation rather than polishing every possible screen.

“The challenge was knowing where additional design effort would matter — and where it wouldn’t.”

The challenge

An early-stage product can absorb unlimited design time if every flow is treated as equally important. CrewInSync needed enough improvement to feel coherent and credible while still respecting startup budget and speed.

What I inherited

The product already had substantial functionality, but the experience was visually dense and fragmented. The redesign needed to make the product easier to scan and understand without spending startup time rebuilding everything.

What I saw

The highest-value work was concentrated in a small number of moments: setting up a role, defining what matters to the team, interviewing candidates and reviewing applications. Those moments also gave me enough surface area to establish a visual system the rest of the product could inherit.

What I decided

I prioritised the product moments that shaped understanding and trust, then stopped where extra refinement would produce diminishing returns. The goal was not a perfect redesign. It was maximum product impact for the available design investment.

What I created

In around 50 hours I redesigned the key hiring-manager and candidate flows, established a clearer visual direction and created a reusable component foundation. Figma became the source of truth for the experience, patterns and states the startup could continue building from.

What changed

The team was left with both a clearer experience and a component system they could continue using. That mattered more than creating a larger volume of one-off screens that would immediately need more design support.

The outcome was not “every screen redesigned.” It was a product that had a clearer direction, a stronger set of core journeys and a reusable visual system the team could continue applying after my 50-hour engagement.
